# Story formula and gag library

## Shape (35-47 s, about 110-130 spoken words at a natural ElevenLabs pace of 2.5-3 words a second)
1. **Hook (0-8 s)**: a relatable situation told fast and funny. The visual is the busiest stretch of the ad.
2. **Problem question** ("so why aren't more people actually buying?"): the audience should nod. The visual freezes or sags.
3. **Silent gag beat (optional, +2 s)**: the over-the-top reaction, placed right after the question. Cut it if it slows the flow.
4. **Reframe (8-10 s)**: the one belief, explained with a visual metaphor (paddle, timeline, meter, thermometer).
5. **Fix (8-10 s)**: your offer, named and shown doing one job. The same characters transform (stern -> smiling, NO -> SOLD).
6. **CTA (6-8 s)**: optional photo badge + chat bubble with the word to message + the free thing (cover with a FREE starburst).

## Worked example beat for beat (this sold a marketing system; replace the offer)
| Line | Visual |

## Gag library (one or two per ad; escalate; one clear image each)
- Rage escalation: rips out a phone or lifts a model, throws it at a wall (wind-up pose, follow-through pose, flying prop, starburst, cracks, shards), then stamps on it (up/down poses + bursts + squash).
- Boss slams a fist on a desk: desk shakes, mug jumps, coffee drops fly.
- Paddle/judge: NO before they speak, SOLD after trust. Flip the sign by swapping the item.
- Speech bubbles ("...") flying at a stone-faced person and bonking off a shield or stop-hand.
- Meter (empty -> full); ice block melting off a cold prospect; chart reading 0; thermometer reading COLD; confetti that stops dead; duct tape over a mouth; smoke from the ears.
- Facing direction matters: a throw must go TOWARD its target. Mirror single poses with `flipPoses`.

## Writing rules
- Audience: the person who holds the budget (marketing manager, owner with a sales team). The humour is a professional operation that looks right on paper but is not closing.
- Describe behaviour in plain words. No jargon the audience does not use.
- One belief per ad. Never promise a result, a number, a price or a guarantee. The story is what you believe, not what will happen.
- Characters speak in character: an angry boss does not praise anyone. Read each line as the person saying it.
- Voice tags go in the script, e.g. `[excited]`, `[dramatic sigh]`, `[dry]`, `[sympathetic]`, `[serious]`, `[calm]`, `[friendly]`. They are not spoken.

## CTA + lead magnet pattern
`Message me the word "X" for our free "Y", and see if your company needs this.`
The free item can be a 2-3 page PDF guide or test. Show its cover as a sticker in the last scene on the words "free ... Y", with a FREE starburst. If you have no cover, build a simple one in HTML and print it to PDF/PNG with headless Chrome.
